Stream Invertebrate Community Survey

I worked with then-undergraduate Rafael Baez and Professor Katie Schneider Paolantonio, to develop leaf packs (a common method of sampling invertebrate biodiversity in fresh-water systems) constructed out of biodegradable materials. We selected Shoelace Park, which borders part of the Bronx River, and is an important green-space for the community. I joined the project during the data collection process, identifying and counting aquatic invertebrates, as well as in quantifying water quality parameters. During the sampling season, we collaborated with members of the Bronx River Alliance, as well as recruited students in Prof. Paolantonios Ecological Field Methods to help out. We are working on writing up our results into a publication.

Morphology of the Invasive Spotted Lanternfly

Working with undergraduate colleagues Hannah Owen and Aria Zhang, both at NYU Shanghai, we sought to investigate the recently-introduced Spotted Lanternfly (Lycorma delicatula). While Hannah focused on studying their phenology in their invasive range, Aria and I sought to investigate their wing morphology. With samples from across their native and invasive ranges, we set out to quantify the wing shape, size, as well as coloration patterns across 3 main regions of both hind and forewings. We are working on writing up our results into a publication.

Luxury Effect Project

In collaboration with Post-doctoral Researcher Valentina Alaasam and a variety of graduate and undergraduate students from the Winchell Lab, We developed a study investigating the effects of socio-economic disparity on the biodiversity of birds and herpetofauna (amphibians and reptiles). This on-going project has surveyed parks in most of the 5 boroughs, and has been a major point of engagement for undergraduates curious about ecological research. I led numerous bird surveys, and am engaged in data-mining from public GIS databases, and analysis.
